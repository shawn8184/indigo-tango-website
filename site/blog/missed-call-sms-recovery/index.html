Stop Quoting With the Bot. Capture the Missed Call.

Trades lose jobs when the phone rings under the hood. Fix it with text-back and human quotes — not unsupervised AI pricing.

A luxury mechanic shop owner was missing more than a hundred calls a week while working under cars. His sister wrote up the build of an AI receptionist meant to answer, quote prices from a knowledge base, and take callbacks when it did not know the answer. The post landed on Hacker News with hundreds of comments (HN discussion; build write-up). The interesting part was not the stack. It was what experienced service advisors said in the replies.

They did not argue that missed calls are fine. They argued that an unsupervised bot quoting brakes or engine work is a liability. Book time, parts availability, state estimate rules, and judgment about related work are not safe for a raw agent. Several people said the useful layer is narrower: answer or text back, capture the vehicle and the complaint, promise a callback from a person who can quote.

Search demand is moving toward the AI version of that coverage. Credminds' July 2026 demand index, built from Google Ads volume estimates for US searches, reports roughly 63,800 trailing-twelve-month searches for "ai receptionist," about triple the prior year, and notes that the phrase overtook "virtual receptionist" in December 2025 (Credminds index). That is interest, not adoption — but buyers are typing the problem into Google.

The pain, said plainly

For HVAC, plumbing, electrical, auto, and other call-heavy local shops, the phone is the front door. When the owner or tech cannot pick up, the caller tries the next listing. Voicemail alone often loses the lead. Full voice AI that invents a price creates a different failure: sticker shock, bad reviews, and estimate-rule risk.

What shops actually need on day one is recovery of the interruption:

  • Detect a missed call or after-hours ring.
  • Send a short SMS within a minute asking for name, service area, and need.
  • Classify urgency (emergency vs. routine).
  • Open a structured lead in the CRM or field-service tool with a transcript.
  • Page the owner for emergencies; queue the rest for human callback and a real quote.

Who this is for

Owners and office managers in home services and similar trades who already lose jobs to voicemail, who have looked at "AI receptionist" pitches and smelled the hype, and who still need something that works while someone is on a ladder. If you need the bot to negotiate scope and price without a human, this is the wrong engagement.

What an Indigo Tango engagement does

We treat missed-call recovery as a done-for-you operations build on the tools you already use.

  • Map the call path. Carrier, CRM or field-service tool, after-hours rules, who gets paged.
  • Build the recovery loop. Missed-call SMS, optional voice greeting that does not quote, intake, emergency escalate.
  • Wire writeback. Leads land as tasks or jobs with source, transcript, and status.
  • Put humans on money. Quoting and formal estimates stay with staff.
  • Tune after go-live. Real callers break happy paths; we stay for the exception queue.

Practitioners have also been writing about rising Google Trends interest in phrases like "ai workflow automation" (Trends write-up). The buyers showing up want repetitive revenue leaks closed — not another toy agent.

What you should not expect

No invented lift percentages. Use your own call logs and close rates in the assessment. Credminds publishes search volumes with clear methodology limits. HN commenters disagree about luxury fit and hiring alternatives.

Next step

Start with a fixed-scope AI Opportunity Assessment. Book at schedule an assessment or read what the assessment covers. Missed calls will keep going to the next contractor until something answers — make sure what answers knows when to take a message.
