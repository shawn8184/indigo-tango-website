AI Automation for Better Workflows Now

Automation only helps if it survives contact with how your team actually works. Here's what that takes.

The part nobody automates first

Most teams don't have one big process to fix. They have a dozen small ones: the inbox that gets triaged by hand every morning, the spreadsheet that gets re-typed into the CRM, the report that gets assembled the same way every Friday. None of it feels worth automating on its own. All of it adds up to hours a week that never show up on anyone's calendar.

Where AI actually helps here
  • Triage and routing — sorting incoming work by what it is and who should see it, before a person has to open every item to find out.
  • Drafting, not deciding — a first-pass reply, summary, or entry that a person reviews and sends, instead of a blank page every time.
  • Moving data between systems — the same information typed once instead of three times across email, spreadsheet, and CRM.
  • Flagging what's off — catching the order that doesn't match a pattern or the invoice that's missing a field, before it becomes a problem downstream.
What it doesn't do

It doesn't replace the judgment calls — the exception that needs a human decision, the customer conversation that needs a person, the approval that carries real risk if it's wrong. The Assessment is where we draw that line for your specific business, not a generic one.

"The goal isn't fewer people. It's fewer hours spent on work that was never the job in the first place."
Where to start

Start with the AI Opportunity Assessment: two weeks, flat fee, a prioritized list of what to automate first, what to augment with AI, and what stays human. You get a real plan before anything gets built — not a tool subscription and a hope.
